# Grammar and vocabulary mastery

## Grammar targeting

Every lesson is classified from its actual Grammar title/focus into one of 12 language-function categories: tense/aspect, question/word order, noun/article/quantity, comparison, modal/condition, clause/linking, passive/reported, discourse/cohesion, hedging/stance, interaction/repair, paraphrase/explanation, or integrated grammar.

The category limits the operation family. Discourse work uses linking, paraphrase, or cumulative retrieval—not a generic subject/tense swap. Hedging calibrates claims against evidence. Interaction creates a clarification/repair exchange. Explanation preserves meaning while changing wording. Days 1–30 remain controlled/guided/contextual even when the category's later operation set includes error correction.

Error correction now starts from a curated Japanese-learner pattern. It never asks the learner to invent a wrong sentence. High-value cases cover articles/countability, tense/aspect, indirect-question order, conditionals, modal form, relative clauses, passive/reported speech, cohesion, hedging strength, and pragmatic clarification. The learner sees the incorrect form, correction, and reason before producing a contextual sentence that avoids the error.

## Curriculum lexical reuse

This layer supplements—not replaces—SRS. A daily block includes current targets and at most two previously introduced targets:

- D+1: immediate recall in a sentence
- D+3: contextual/collocation reuse
- D+7: productive example with current language
- D+21: paraphrase plus an integrating sentence

D+1 is always selected after Day 1; one longer interval rotates across D+3/D+7/D+21. A target is read only from an earlier bundled lesson, so pre-introduction retrieval is impossible. Reused terms do not enter acquisition counts and do not change review scheduling, learning-item state, or Core evidence.

## Foundation communicative calibration

The 15 Foundation Labs retain their texts, tasks, IDs, and order. Output ranges now follow the task rather than a mechanical day curve. A polite request or ingredient question can succeed in 4–16 words; directions, messages, comparisons, and repair exchanges receive a larger range; the Day 90 reflection remains a 45–100-word paragraph. Difficulty still rises through language function and connected output, while irrelevant padding is no longer rewarded.
